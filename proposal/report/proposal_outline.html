Proposal outline: Data leakage in flight-delay prediction

1. Research question

  • Main question: How much does information unavailable two hours before scheduled departure influence prediction of arrival delay?
  • Focus: Investigate how using information unavailable at the prediction time changes model performance, comparing a clean predictor set with predefined leakage conditions.

2. Data and background

Background and motivation

  • Existing problem: Why it matters? How is it connected to our study**
  • Relevant studies

Data sources

  • BTS Reporting Carrier On-Time Performance: Provides flight related information: flight dates, airlines, origin/destination airports, schedules, distance, arrival-delay outcomes, etc..
  • Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM) METAR/ASOS archive: Provides airport-level weather observations: match wind speed and precipitation to corresponding flight locations and times.

Full-study scope

  • Locations: U.S. domestic flights across airports availably represented in the BTS dataset, with weather matched at both origin and destination.
  • Development (training + validation) period: January–June 2025, including chronological validation for selection and tuning.
  • Final evaluation (test) period: July-September 2025, final testing only.
  • Eligible flights: Completed, non-diverted flights with available observed outcome.

Data Structure

  • Outcome: Whether arrival is at least 15 minutes late, represented by ArrDel15.
  • Prediction time (cutoff): Two hours prior to the scheduled departure.
  • Leakage Variables:
    • Weather leakage: wind measurements and accumulated precipitation amounts taken near the scheduled departure time.
    • Operational leakage: DepDelay and TaxiOut.
  • Candidate Predictors:
    • Predictors from source datasets: scheduled departure time, scheduled duration, distance, airline, origin/destination, weekday, and weather measurements.
    • Derived predictors: holiday indicators, historical delay rates.

3. Proposed comparison and primary estimands

This project will use two models and three feature conditions for comparison.

  • Model Selection: Logistic regression and Random Forest
  • Feature conditions: “Clean condition,” which only contains planning information, and “Deliberate leakage condition,” which adds unavailable information. The “deliberate leakage condition” includes two levels: weather leakage and weather + operational leakage.
Condition Weather and operational information
Clean (C) Information on wind speed and precipitation at the takeoff and landing sites that is available before the cutoff; operational information that is available before the cutoff.
Weather leakage (W) Includes subsequent wind speed measurements and cumulative precipitation values ​​close to the planned takeoff time based on feature set C.
Weather + operational leakage (WO) Based on feature set W, further includes unavailable operational information such as DepDelay(departure delay) and TaxiOut(taxiing time)

  • Primary metric: Average Precision (AP) summarizes precision across prediction thresholds, weighted by the corresponding increases in sensitivity. It measures how well the model ranks delayed flights above on-time flights and is useful when the outcome classes are imbalanced. Higher AP indicates better model performance.

  • Secondary metric: Brier score: The Brier score is the mean squared error between the predicted probability and the actual binary observation. It is introduced to measure the accuracy and calibration of the model’s output probability. A lower Brier score reflects better model performance.

  • The primary estimand is the difference in performance between the leakage conditions and the clean condition.

    Delta_AP = AP_leakage - AP_clean.

4. Evaluation plan

Data Split: To ensure a robust and realistic assessment, we will implement a chronological data split prior to the final evaluation. The proposed annual allocation for the 2025 flight data is: * Development Set: Jan.–Jun. * Final Test Set: Jul.–Sep.

Model Development and Control: Use only development data for preprocessing fitting (e.g. standardization) and budget-capped hyperparameter tuning. All configurations must be frozen before testing, and clean condition (C) features must be strictly limited to 2 hours before takeoff.

Paired comparison: The clean (C), weather leakage (W), and weather and operational leakage (WO) conditions must be paired and evaluated on the same test flights using equivalent parameter tuning budgets.

Evaluation: Assess AP and Brier score, and report the delay rate and a constant probability reference benchmark based on the development set.

Uncertainty: Perform paired resampling for the test date to estimate paired confidence intervals for performance metric differences. If the time dependency between dates is significant, longer time blocks will be considered for resampling. It’s important to clarify that this method primarily addresses the variation in results caused by different evaluation dates (based on a fixed-fit model); however, there are still other training uncertainties that remain unaccounted for.

Interpretation: When we compare clean (C) and weather leakage (W), both time and the standards for measuring the weather have changed; both factors influence the process, so we cannot assert that the model improvement is because of time. To compensate for the inaccuracies above, we further introduce weather and operational leakage (WO) to compare W and WO. In this case, weather conditions are frozen, making the operational conditions the only factor influencing the model; so any difference in the accuracy of the two predictions can be attributed entirely to the “leakage of operational information.”

5. Minimum viable experiment

We conducted a preliminary experiment using flights departing from Atlanta in January 2025. Model fitting uses 14,093 flights from January 1 to 20 and the performance was evaluated on 3,636 validation flights from January 21 to 25. The test set contains 4,851 flights from January 26 to 31, which have remained untouched. Weather observations were matched to the corresponding flights at the departure airport.

The preliminary validation results and their interpretation are provided in Appendix A. The completed data matching, model fitting, and evaluation demonstrates the feasibility of our proposed experiment.

The minimum viable experiment compares both models under the three information conditions using a sample limited in time and geographic coverage. The full study will expand coverage to IEM-covered airports across the United States and include weather conditions at destination airports.

6. Computing requirements and verified access to data and code

We will use R and the ranger package on a CPU-based laptop. Data will be downloaded and processed in a month unit.

Access to the required BTS monthly files has been verified, and IEM weather data have been verified for selected airports. The preliminary analysis has confirmed that flight and weather data can be combined for model fitting. Information availability, and computing requirements, including runtime and memory use, will be checked further upon the full study.

7. Fallback and responsibilities

If computing limits require sampling, we will sample proportionally within each month and origin airport. Sample coverage will be checked, and weights will be applied if flights have unequal selection probabilities. Further fallbacks include shortening historical-delay-rate windows from 365 to 182 days and reducing tuning or tree budgets. Forecast data are an option for investigating the association between weather and flight delays.

Appendix A. Preliminary pilot results

Table A1 presents the results of the untuned models on the same 3,636 validation flights departing from Atlanta from January 21 to 25, 2025. The January 26–31 test set was not evaluated.

Table A1. Pilot validation performance under three information conditions.

Model Clean Weather leakage only Weather + operational leakage
Logistic regression 0.2291 / 0.2776 0.2261 / 0.2777 0.9764 / 0.0389
Random forest 0.3509 / 0.2570 0.4286 / 0.2421 0.9710 / 0.0414

Each cell reports AP / Brier, where higher AP and lower Brier indicate better performance.

The weather-leakage condition improved random forest performance but produced little change in logistic regression performance. Adding departure delay and taxi-out duration produced much larger apparent gains for both models.
